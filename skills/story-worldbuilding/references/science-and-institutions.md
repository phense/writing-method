# Scientific claims and institutional consequences

Use for technically grounded fiction or a research-heavy novel study. This is this method's provisional craft synthesis; technical plausibility and literary effectiveness are different claims.

Provenance: own analysis from private studies of existing works; the private study notes are not distributed. See the [source index](../../../shared/story/sources.md#study-derived-references).

## Separate four layers

For a load-bearing concept record the fictional claim and exact passage, the real mechanism and primary source, the invented departure, and its consequences for characters. Preserve publication date, story date and research date. A later invention may be a useful comparison without proving historical availability.

Label a narrator statement, character hypothesis, advertised result, measured result and analyst inference differently. A quoted success percentage needs its task, sample and measurement boundary before it becomes a capability. Fast replies do not establish consciousness; absence of a detected signal does not establish absence of every process. Explain only the distinction relevant to the scene.

Keep mixed fiction/science cards inside the story or study vault they belong to; where a memory or retrieval tool is used, they stay in that vault's scope under the [retrieval contract](../../../shared/story/retrieval.md). Only separately written, attributed general technical notes belong in the general craft scope. Include inspected source scope and unresolved assumptions. A source-backed mechanism does not certify the whole fictional device. An inaccessible primary source leaves the claim open.

## Make a capability consequential

Trace capability → required resource/access → responsible institution → local incentive → action → downstream cost. Ask where someone must grant access, maintain hardware, believe a message, tolerate an outage or honor a contract. Ordinary competent work can create unintended consequences without turning every employee into an idiot.

A fictional power needs relevant boundaries, not an exhaustive engineering specification. Show maintenance, time, coordination and ownership when they create meaningful options. If physical realism is not the chosen contract, preserve the agreed speculative rule instead of interrupting every scene with correction.

## Build reversals from a limited model

Record what a test actually observes and what the characters infer. Prepare a meaningful missing dependency before using it to undo a victory. The reader need not predict the reversal, but should be able to reconstruct its cause. Do not invent an unlimited hidden backup whenever a protagonist succeeds.

When public benefit conflicts with private harm, identify who chooses, who benefits, who pays and who can refuse. Different characters may support the same outcome for different reasons. Neither a benefit ledger nor a final sinister image settles every ethical question automatically.

## Separate capacity from active attention

For a distributed or nonhuman mind, distinguish available sensors, stored information, active attention and authority to act. When an important signal is missed, locate the failure in that chain: a queue, an interpretation, a competing task or a decision to defer. Prepare the relevant selection rule before relying on it. This creates a specific blind spot without silently reducing established intelligence. Avoid turning every mind into a computer scheduler; use the equivalent boundary appropriate to the premise.

For unequal experienced time, use the [existing scene questions](../../story-drafting/references/serial-scene-review.md#unequal-clocks-and-uneven-competence). At the institutional level, additionally ask whose response interval sets a deadline and what silence means to each party. A timeout may exclude a willing participant or falsely suggest refusal. If cooperation matters, give the institution a plausible way to acknowledge waiting, preserve a proposal or appoint a proxy; do not assume faster participants are automatically wiser or slower ones helpless.

## Follow a decision through delivery

When a settlement promises a functioning service, distinguish authorization, implementation and the recipient's observed result. Find the relevant remaining dependency: missing interface knowledge, trained operators, a retained bargaining advantage or disagreement among implementers. Show the dependency that changes the next choice, rather than inventing a mandatory obstacle after every agreement. A well-prepared institution may deliver immediately; then let the successful delivery change trust or available options.

## Check who can answer the ending

When an ending grants forgiveness, assigns blame or celebrates recovery after institutional harm, compare the speaking cast with the groups whose interests the story previously made legible. An absent group cannot automatically be treated as consenting, compensated or morally irrelevant. Choose whether the ending acknowledges that limit, supplies a surviving trace or leaves the issue deliberately unresolved. This is a viewpoint-coverage check, not a requirement for a new viewpoint or a courtroom scene; a deliberately partial or unreliable ending may preserve the exclusion. When a victorious protagonist repeats an antagonist's coercive action, temporarily compare the actions without names or celebratory framing: whose options were removed, what consent existed, and what relevant circumstances differed? Restore those differences before judging. The comparison tests narrative favoritism; it does not imply that superficially similar acts have identical motives, stakes or moral weight.

## Give an unfamiliar capability a usable representation

When a character must act inside an unfamiliar system, distinguish reducing information speed from changing how options are represented. A familiar craft can make a choice legible if its operations map onto the new task: what corresponds to an action, what feedback indicates its effect, and where the analogy stops working. Demonstrate one decision changed by that mapping. A useful representation does not itself supply missing access, knowledge or physical power; establish those separately. Avoid importing an entire specialist vocabulary when a plain action would be clearer.

If a change in embodiment or cognition motivates a formal viewpoint shift, choose the smallest device that conveys the change: pronouns, sensory range, sentence organization or narrative distance. Retain an identifiable person, unfinished choice or physical sensation across the transition, unless temporary disorientation is intentional. Test whether the reader can identify whose experience continues and what the new form reveals that the old one could not. A changed capability need not require changed grammar; formal novelty is not proof of transformed consciousness.

## Preserve provenance when a person returns

When copying, resurrection, memory repair or reconstruction carries an emotional payoff, record the source state, missing interval, later divergence and any filled-in material for each relevant person. Distinguish a technically functioning return, the returned person's self-understanding and another character's acceptance. A reunion can succeed relationally while remaining incomplete informationally; do not silently convert recognition into proof that nothing was lost. Prepare the specific limitation before making it decisive, unless the work deliberately withholds it. This is an internal consistency aid, not a demand to settle metaphysical identity or expose every reconstruction detail to readers. A premise of exact magical restoration may simply honor that promise.

## Carry an escalation across different observers

For a consequential chain involving opposed institutions, track the initiating act, the signal visible elsewhere, its interpretation and the authorized response. A viewpoint change earns causal work when it shows how the same event acquires a different operational meaning. Locate the missing or misleading information, and ask whether an available clarification would realistically arrive in time; avoid keeping conflict alive only because competent characters inexplicably refuse an easy conversation. Preserve deliberate aggression and differing values where they matter: not every escalation is a misunderstanding, and better information need not produce agreement. Show only the handoff that changes the next choice, rather than replaying every event in full.

## Unbundle a capability from its imposed restrictions

When a pivotal world rule blocks a choice, distinguish the physical mechanism, a genuine system dependency, an institutional restriction and a character's assumption about necessity. A useful reversal can preserve a capability while changing a restriction, if earlier effects gave the reader grounds to understand that separation. Do not reveal a convenient removable switch for a limit previously established as intrinsic. Some worlds deliberately make the rule indivisible; respect that contract. This is a selective design check for consequential constraints, not a requirement to explain every mechanism.

## Research before asking the author

Resolve factual questions from suitable primary sources. Ask the author when a decision changes the chosen plausibility contract, a pivotal outcome or accepted canon: preserve an inaccurate source-world rule, deliberately depart from it, or choose a different mechanism. Offer concrete consequences, retain prior answers, and continue independent work. Do not ask permission for routine source checking or silently turn a proposed correction into canon.
