# Guided development from an idea to a finished story

Use this workflow for developing the author's own fiction. It orchestrates the nine story skills; it is not an additional software lifecycle or a requirement to complete an encyclopedia before writing. Read the [stage map](workflow.md) for category coverage and storage, and the [working rules](rules.md) for canon boundaries. Resume from the current artifacts, not from a fresh interview.

This file is the only orchestration authority for the story family. The family is not part of the lifecycle handoff graph: no story skill hands off to, or is handed work by, a skill outside the family, with the one exception stated under [narrated video](#narrated-video-after-acceptance), and the passes below overlap and return instead of running as a linear lifecycle. The family is experimental; nothing here has been piloted on a finished story.

## The conversation loop

At each turn:

1. Read the brief, current development state, relevant accepted notes and prior decisions. Extract decisions already stated in the conversation, including delegated choices. Do not ask them again.
2. Identify the next usable artifact and the few dependencies it actually needs. Show concrete progress before asking the author to approve a direction.
3. If a consequential choice is missing, ask one self-contained question, or at most three closely related questions. Explain what the answer changes. Offer two or three genuinely different options with costs and an informed recommendation; free-form answers remain possible. Do not hide several decisions in a large option bundle.
4. Continue independent work while an answer is pending. Wait before committing dependent prose or canon. Silence is not approval. If the author has delegated this class of choice, choose within that delegation and record the reason; do not impose a mandatory question anyway.
5. Record the result, its status and affected artifacts. End with what is now usable, the next step and any still-pending decision. Avoid repeating the complete questionnaire.

A recommendation is not an accepted decision. Label alternatives and assumptions as proposed. Accept material only under the author's stated working agreement; routine drafting authorization is not permission to rewrite published canon. When taste is unclear, a small contrasted sample is often more useful than abstract terminology.

## Which questions deserve interruption?

| Decision | Ask when unresolved | Usually proceed without asking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reader promise | Competing emotional outcomes would create different books: comforting romance versus relationship tragedy, wonder versus sustained dread | Summarize the promise already expressed; ask only about an actual conflict |
| Ending | Choice changes the central relationship, protagonist fate or moral outcome | Keep distant events provisional under an agreed discovery approach |
| Voice | Language, viewpoint, tense or distance materially changes requested prose and no preference/delegation exists | Apply an established voice sheet; propose a tiny reversible sample |
| World rule | The choice changes available solutions, central identity, agency or the plausibility contract | Draft a bounded causal option; defer irrelevant geography and names |
| Character | Incompatible motives or trajectories require an author preference | Develop motives and supporting details consistent with the brief |
| Scope | Chapter, arc or volume estimates imply a materially different project; the publication profile is needed for the next planning step and still unset | Under the web-serial profile use approximately 100 chapters per sub-arc as the maintainer's default, with meaningful movements rather than padding; under other profiles leave figures open |
| Canon conflict | A change would contradict accepted facts, erase a payoff or revise published work | Record the conflict and continue unaffected research; do not silently retcon |
| Local detail | Ask only if it carries thematic or canon consequences | Choose a provisional name, prop or transition and keep it revisable |

Good question: “Should the central romance remain a reliable refuge during the political conflict, or should their loyalty to each other be the main uncertainty? This changes the source of tension in the first arc.” Bad question: “Tell me everything about the world before we begin.”

## Development passes and usable outputs

| Pass | Work and output | Focused author decision | Smallest useful next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Seed | Capture the supplied idea in the brief. State protagonist desire, disruptive situation, emotional promise and one central incompatibility. If necessary offer two or three short interpretations, without choosing a first novel during method work. | Which experience or interpretation should lead, if the supplied idea allows incompatible directions? | One-paragraph premise, confirmed requirements and open options |
| B. Story engine | Combine desire, obstacle, consequential choice and changed conditions. Find repeatable sources of episodes: work, exploration, relationships, obligations or opposition. Repetition must allow development. | Which conflict is central when alternatives imply different books? | A causal spine with an intended ending or explicit discovery strategy |
| C. World and past | Build the distinct feature, its limits, everyday consequences, institutions, peoples and the pre-story events needed to explain the opening. Separate species from culture, facts from beliefs, event time from reveal time. | Which rule or historical truth changes the story's moral or practical possibilities? | A small playable world, a causal history chain and marked expandable gaps |
| D. Cast and relationships | Design protagonist and supporting cast through wants, fears, competencies, limits, conflicting loyalties and attention. Give important supporting figures decisions of their own. Start a voice card for each speaking character. Revisit B/C when they expose a stronger conflict. | Which incompatible trajectory matters to the author? | Character notes with voice cards, relationship changes and who knows what |
| E. Voice trial | Choose provisional narrative settings. Write a short scene doing a real job in the proposed story. If needed compare two brief treatments of the same beat while keeping events stable. Turn the author's response into the voice sheet: narration contract per viewpoint with viewpoint, tense, distance and permitted shifts; tone palette with baseline, permitted excursions and excluded tones; language conventions; two to four anchor passages, at first the trial passages the author chose, referenced by voice trial record and revision; deliberate exceptions; paratext register; change log. Name at least two anchors: two locations in the trial scene, or the chosen parts of both treatments. | Which concrete passage better matches the desired experience, and why? | The accepted voice sheet V1 with `voice_revision: V1`, `viewpoint`, `tense` and `distance` set in its frontmatter, recorded as an author decision; candidate prose stays proposed as manuscript and canon, and the chosen passages serve as voice references only. Lifecycle of the sheet: [voice and tone](voice-and-tone.md) |
| F. Story architecture | Read form and publication profile. Outline whole-story turns, main and sub-arcs, carried subplots and payoff windows. Estimate scope from intended chapter length where the author has set one. Break the nearest arc into smaller causal movements. Place volume boundaries only where the profile uses volumes. | Resolve incompatible ending promises or excessive scope; not every chapter beat | Whole-story spine, nearest arc and next few chapter cards |
| G. Three-chapter pilot | Draft, revise and compare three consecutive chapters using accepted dependencies. Test voice persistence, relationships, world consequences and state handoff. | Ask for reader feedback on specific confusing, compelling or emotionally wrong passages | A revised opening and concrete revisions to the plan; no claim of 100-chapter reliability |
| H. Manuscript cycles | Draft the next agreed unit from its context packet. Revise causal/motivational problems, then scene movement and prose. Then run the canon review and, as a voice review checkpoint, the voice review of the unit or range against the voice sheet revision in force and the speaking characters' voice cards. Neither review edits accepted prose. After the author accepts changes, update the vault and explicitly refresh scoped retrieval derivatives where a memory tool is used. | Ask only at a new consequential fork or conflict with prior decisions; a voice finding classed as a candidate sheet change is an author decision | Growing manuscript with current state, setups, relationship shifts, recorded acceptance and next context packet |
| I. Arc and whole-work revision | At arc boundaries inspect local closure and changed conditions; at volume boundaries, where volumes are used, check what the volume closes. At the ending compare fulfilled promises with those actually established. Review the complete manuscript for motivation, pacing, canon and voice before line cleanup. | Resolve deliberate open endings, substantial cuts or accepted/published changes | Complete revised manuscript with unresolved limitations recorded; publication remains a separate request |

These passes can overlap. A scene can reveal that a world rule is unusable; a supporting character can expose a missing historical cause. Return to the smallest affected decision, preserve accepted facts and propagate changes to dependent outlines. Do not restart all stages.

Drafting in pass G or H requires a vault of kind story and a voice sheet whose `viewpoint`, `tense` and `distance` are set. When any of the three is still unset, the work returns to pass E. The voice review declines in the same way when the sheet has fewer than two anchor passages or an empty tone palette. Pass E can supply both: the trial passages the author chose are the first anchors, and accepted chapter passages replace or supplement them later.

## How we judge the work

Separate three readings: story causality and emotional payoff; prose execution against the chosen voice; the author's actual taste. Use the [quality lenses](../../skills/story-drafting/references/story-and-prose-quality.md) and the appropriate [serial scene questions](../../skills/story-drafting/references/serial-scene-review.md). Select the questions relevant to the current problem, not every lens for every paragraph.

For feedback, ask for concrete evidence: where did interest drop, what did the reader expect, whose choice was unclear, which line felt out of character? “Make it better” does not establish a measurable target. A model's self-review can identify defects but cannot certify human reader enjoyment. Do not substitute votes among agents for actual author feedback.

Feedback from the author or a beta reader about facts, chronology or knowledge goes to the canon review; feedback about how the text sounds goes to the voice review. Both are judged against the vault, not accepted on the reader's word alone.

## Completion

A chapter is complete when a canon review exists at a named revision and the author's acceptance of that revision is recorded. Acceptance is an author decision, not a command result, so no pass ends in a pass claim. `verification-before-completion` applies only to checks that have a command: link checks, template integrity, an export. A chapter keeps the voice sheet revision it was accepted under; a later sheet change does not reopen it by itself.

## Narrated video after acceptance

A narrated video is an optional delivery of accepted text, never a pass of its own and never a condition for completion. It starts only when the author asks for it and the units in scope have a recorded acceptance at a named revision.

1. `story-video-scripting` asks the author for the edition. Verbatim is the default: the accepted manuscript, segmented and otherwise untouched. A listening edition exists only by author decision, and every difference from the manuscript is recorded as one.
2. The written manuscript is not changed by either edition, and a listening edition is not canon. When reading aloud exposes a problem in the story itself, the work returns to pass H for a revision and a new acceptance.
3. The author accepts the script; that is a recorded decision, not a command result.
4. The accepted script goes to the form skill `presentation-video-production`, and its acceptance review is `presentation-video-review`. This is the only handoff out of the story family. The form skills voice, render and check the script as it is; a finding about wording returns to `story-video-scripting` and the author.

Record the edition, the revisions and the acceptance in the decisions note. The step is experimental, and no video has been produced with it. Delivery as an e-book stays future work.

## Durable handoff

In each real story vault, maintain `00-project/development.md` for the current pass, available artifact, next action, pending question and dependency boundaries. Keep `00-project/decisions.md` for stable decision IDs, author/delegation evidence, status and consequences. Both are inert planning notes, not Loreweaver character or foreshadow entities. A story vault gets no backlog; these two notes hold its state.

Do not turn the author into a form-filler. Update these notes from the conversation. Keep only useful decisions and clear open questions. On a later return, show the outstanding question with enough context to answer it without rereading the whole project. If the author changes direction, identify impacted files and mark superseded choices; do not erase the decision history.
