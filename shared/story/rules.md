# Working rules

These are initial craft heuristics and workflow requirements, not proven predictors of readership. The story family is experimental: no rule here has been piloted on a finished story. Source IDs resolve in [the source index](sources.md). Author choices override defaults.

For manuscript text the order of authority is: the story's voice sheet, then these rules. How the voice sheet is built and changed is described in [voice and tone](voice-and-tone.md).

## #1 storytelling rule: therefore / but

**Connect consequential story beats through cause and consequence or complication: this happens, therefore that follows; but something obstructs or changes the course, therefore someone responds.** A beat is a meaningful action, decision or change. A sequence joined only by "and then" needs a stronger story connection. Apply this rule first when planning, drafting and revising a story. (CW06)

For each major transition, name what follows from the preceding action or what obstructs the current intention, and how that changes the next choice. When the connection is missing, repair the motivation, event or order; inserting "therefore" or "but" cannot create causality by itself. Complications may arise independently, but must affect the ongoing situation and produce a response.

This is a structural check, not a requirement to repeat those words in manuscript prose, alternate them mechanically or turn every paragraph into a crisis. Quiet scenes can carry consequences through trust, knowledge, recovery or changed intentions. Nonlinear narration can reveal causes later; distinguish event causality from reveal order.

Attribution: Trey Parker and Matt Stone's NYU craft advice, as presented in the [excerpt recorded as CW06](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/2v3Z9OTC4dA). **Its position as rule #1 is a maintainer requirement of 2026-11-12.** The operational checks and long-fiction qualifications above are this method's synthesis; no empirical reader-quality result is claimed. See [CW06 in the source index](sources.md#cw06-inspected-scope) for inspected scope.

## #1 prose rule: natural prose

**No manuscript sentence may read as machine-written.** [Writing rule #1](../policies/natural-prose.md) lists the patterns and why readers recognize them; its position is a maintainer requirement of 2026-11-13. The storytelling rule above governs how beats connect, this rule how sentences are built; neither replaces the other.

The voice sheet stays the first authority. A device the sheet names, such as fragments, a repeated opening or a list of three used for rhythm, is the author's choice and no finding. Where the sheet is silent, staged insight, inflation in place of a concrete perception, and anything left over from a chat are defects in narration. A character may speak in such a pattern when the character's voice card supports it.

## Further craft heuristics

2. Build enough world to support the next meaningful story decision. Mark other questions open. Wrede's questionnaire is selective; completeness is not a prerequisite for prose. (WB01, WB12, WB14)
3. Trace consequences of a distinctive premise into ordinary life, institutions and personal choices. A feature should produce opportunities and costs beyond a visual gimmick. (WB02, WB05, WB11)
4. Define what magic or technology can do, cannot do, costs, and how people obtain it. When it resolves conflict, prepare the reader for the relevant capability. Mystery may remain mysterious without conveniently solving the climax. (WB03, WB04, WB05)
5. Give inhabitants food, energy, shelter and ecological constraints appropriate to their biology. Research real mechanisms when accuracy matters; mark invented departures. (WB06, WB15, WB17, WB19)
6. Distinguish species, ancestry, culture, nationality, faction and individual identity. Give cultures internal disagreement and change over time. Avoid treating a whole people as one temperament. (WB02; terminology separation is this method's design choice)
7. Make history causal: event, interested parties, immediate outcome, lasting consequence and present conflict. Keep disputed accounts separate from author-level truth. (WB07, WB08)
8. Economics includes time, labor, access, information and incentives. Trace scarcity and distribution rather than inventing currency names alone. (WB09, WB11, WB15)
9. Render beliefs through practices, institutions and individual experience; allow variation within a religion. (WB10)
10. Choose the story's plausibility contract. Hard science, mythic fantasy and science fantasy need different explanations; internal consistency matters in each. (WB13, WB16, WB17, WB18)
11. Reveal world details where the viewpoint character notices, needs, misunderstands or acts on them. Preserve intentional narrator explanation and restful passages when they serve the chosen voice. (WB02, WB12, WB16)
12. Plan at increasing resolution, revisiting earlier decisions when characters reveal a better story. Snowflake is one optional technique, not a required plot formula. (CW01)
13. Treat serial delivery as sustained work. Choose a feasible cadence and a chapter buffer after measuring actual drafting/revision effort. Wildbow's 12–16 chapter suggestion is his experience, not a universal quota. (CW02)

## Maintainer defaults and local design choices

These are requirements and defaults of this method, not source-backed craft advice. The author of a story may override any default; record the departure in the vault's decisions note.

- Every story gets its own vault, kept as an independent Git repository. Fantasy and science fiction have equal standing.
- Cover idea, world, history, species/peoples, necessary pre-story interactions, distinctive features, main/supporting characters, overall arc and sub-arcs. Categories are review lenses, not a command to fill irrelevant encyclopedic fields.
- For the web-serial publication profile, the maintainer's default is approximately 100 chapters per sub-story arc. Record any purposeful departure. At 2,000 words per chapter this would mean about 200,000 words per arc; this is arithmetic, not a recommended chapter length. No other publication profile has a default figure in this method; its numbers are fields the author fills in the volume template.
- Maintain a whole-story destination or explicit open ending strategy, then detail the nearest arc. Do not freeze thousands of chapter beats before testing the voice.
- Keep `story_time` separate from chapter/reveal order, and world truth separate from what each character and the reader knows.
- Review new draft facts before acceptance. For every accepted change, update affected notes and the change log; where a memory or retrieval tool is used, flag its derivatives for refresh.
- A canon review report cites conflicting passages. Missing retrieval evidence means unknown, not false. A state table can verify recorded facts, not automatically prove the full prose consistent.
- Revise causality and character choices before sentence polishing. Retain intentional repetition, fragments, dialect and metaphor when they belong to the narrator or character.
- Evaluate prose through reading and author feedback. No claim of being undetectable by AI detectors or guaranteed to sound human.
- Unresolved real-world research never becomes asserted science because the fictional setting needs it.
- Accepting a chapter is an author decision recorded in the vault. No command result, review report or agent vote stands in for it.
