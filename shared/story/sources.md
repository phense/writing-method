# Source index for the story family

This is the attribution index for every source ID cited in the story rules and skills, and for the references that were derived from studying existing works. It is a compact index, not the research corpus: the full source cards and the study notes are private working material of the maintainer and are not distributed with this method. New source cards use the [source card template](../../templates/story-vault/90-templates/source-card.md).

The sources are distinct resources, not independent experimental studies. Several Writing Excuses episodes and Sanderson essays overlap; agreement among them is not independent validation. Craft advice is not empirical proof of literary quality. No source text is copied here; each row gives only the takeaway this method adopted.

## Kinds of evidence

| Kind | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| page | The page or PDF was retrieved and assessed for the stated takeaway. It does not mean an entire course or series was completed. |
| search-excerpt | Only an indexed excerpt was assessed. Weaker evidence; it may not support detailed rules. |
| video-storyboard-captions | Sampled video frames and their embedded captions were visually inspected. Not continuous viewing and not a retrieved subtitle transcript. |

All rows were checked on 2026-10-15 except CW06, checked on 2026-11-12.

## CW06: inspected scope

The source is a Short of roughly 71 seconds from the speakers' NYU classroom appearance. The watch page and its metadata were retrieved, and all eight storyboard sheets were visually inspected. The embedded on-screen captions show the outline and beat discussion, the warning about "and then", the alternatives "therefore" and "but", and the explanation of consequence and complication in contrast with a mere list of events. These are sampled frames with embedded captions, not continuous viewing and not a retrieved subtitle transcript. The full lecture was not inspected, and the Short's publication date was not verified.

As a cross-check, Scott Myers's [2011 report of the NYU appearance](https://gointothestory.blcklst.com/matt-stone-and-trey-parker-at-nyu-e0197747e74f/) was read; it quotes the same rule and discusses consequences and complications. That report corroborates the advice; it is not a transcript of the Short.

What the source supports: link outline beats through consequences or complications; a chronological list lacks the causal movement the speakers seek. Their example concerns screen storytelling and their own writing process. It is author craft advice, not empirical evidence about reader enjoyment.

What is this method's own: the position as rule #1, which is a maintainer requirement of 2026-11-12, and the operational checks for long fiction in [the working rules](rules.md#1-storytelling-rule-therefore--but). The rule is not a ban on the words "and then", a demand for constant conflict or a mechanical alternation of connectives. Its usefulness for long fiction written with this method has not been tested in a story pilot.
