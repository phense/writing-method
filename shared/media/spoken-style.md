# Writing for listening

Narration is heard once, at the speaker's pace, while the viewer also looks at
a picture. Text that reads well on a page can fail here. These rules apply
from the first draft, together with
[writing rule #1](../policies/natural-prose.md) and
[human-prose-drafting](../../skills/human-prose-drafting/SKILL.md).

## One line, one thought

- A narration line is one sentence or two short ones that belong together.
  The profile sets the limits (default: 28 words per line; a sentence above
  22 words draws a warning). The three slowest of the measured videos were
  also the ones with the longest lines.
- Put the new information at the end of the sentence, where the voice lands.
- Introduce at most one new term per line, and say what it is in the same
  line or the next.
- A line matches what is on screen while it is spoken. If the picture needs
  longer than the sentence, give the line a minimum display time; do not pad
  the sentence.

## Say it the way it is spoken

- Write numbers, units and dates the way a person says them. "About nine
  minutes" beats "8:54 min".
- An abbreviation needs a spoken form: an entry in the pronunciation list
  ("IT" spoken "I T"), or the full words.
- No brackets, slashes, lists or "see above". The listener cannot look back.
- The caption may be spelled differently from the spoken form. It never says
  something else.

## Friendly, not hyped

The tone is that of a helpful colleague: warm, confident, moderately positive.

| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Address the viewer directly, in the form of address the script names | Passive constructions and "the user" |
| Confirm success after a step: "That's it, the entry is saved." | Praise for trivial actions |
| Open with what the viewer will be able to do | Opening with apologies or disclaimers |
| One friendly closing sentence | Exclamation chains, superlatives, sales language |
| Name a difficulty plainly, then the way through it | Alarm words for ordinary pitfalls |

Most voices take their tone from the words. A flat script produces flat
speech; friendly wording is the first and most reliable control. Provider
settings come second and only after they were shown to work for the voice
(see [speech synthesis](speech-synthesis.md)).

## Tell the truth about the picture

When interfaces are recreated or people and data are invented, say so once
near the start, in plain words: "The windows in this video are redrawn. On
your screen, details can look slightly different."

## Length

A tutorial video is as long as its content needs. Do not cut or split to reach
a short target. The profile sets one maximum (default 45 minutes); a script
above it becomes two videos, split at a chapter boundary, each complete on
its own.
